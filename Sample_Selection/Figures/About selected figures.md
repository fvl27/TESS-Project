### Figure 1. Planet Sample Selection

This figure shows the minimum planetary mass as a function of orbital period. The color scale represents the spectral type of the host stars, while marker sizes indicate their relative brightness. Gray points represent planets outside the selected sample.

The visualization illustrates the target-selection process used to identify low-mass planets with orbital periods shorter than 30 days. This step helped define a suitable sample for the subsequent search for transit signals in TESS photometric data.


### Figure 2. Photometric Noise Analysis

This figure compares the observed root mean square (RMS) of normalized TESS light curves with the expected RMS for pure Gaussian white noise as a function of bin size. The analysis is shown for three host stars with different apparent brightnesses.

The departure of the observed RMS from the white-noise expectation reveals the presence of correlated noise (red noise) on different timescales. Characterizing this noise is important because it affects the reliability and sensitivity of transit detection.

This analysis illustrates the importance of data quality assessment before applying time-series analysis algorithms.

### Figure 4. Validation of the Transit Detection Method

This figure presents the analysis of 55 Cancri e, a known transiting super-Earth, used to test the transit-search methodology.

The left panel shows the Box Least Squares (BLS) periodogram, with a prominent peak at the known orbital period and its harmonics. The middle panel displays the phase-folded light curve using the period and phase recovered by BLS, together with synthetic transit models. The right panel shows the phase-folded light curve using orbital parameters reported in the literature, including the phase uncertainty.

The agreement between the recovered signal, the observed photometry, and the reference transit models supports the reliability of the analysis workflow.
